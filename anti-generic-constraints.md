# Anti-Generic Creative Constraints — Alexa Cera Portfolio
> Companion file to `alexa-cera-portfolio-design-spec.md`
> Purpose: Prevent AI-generated or templated output from feeling safe, averaged, or corporate.
> Use this file alongside the spec to guide decisions about what to resist, break, and preserve.

---

## 1. How It Should Feel

This is not a polished agency deliverable. It should feel like a **confident person designed their own poster** — someone who knows exactly who they are and didn't ask for permission.

- Energetic, not elegant
- Direct, not decorative
- Personal, not professional-neutral
- Like a bold editorial spread, not a SaaS landing page
- The type should feel *slightly too big* — like it almost doesn't fit

If it looks like it could be a Webflow template, it's too generic. If it looks like someone made it for exactly one person, it's right.

---

## 2. Intentional Imbalance Rules

These are deliberate design decisions that may look like "mistakes" — do not correct them.

- **"CERA" overlaps the photo** — it sits on top of the subject's lower body. Do not push it to the side to "fix" the overlap. The overlap is the point.
- **"ALEXA" and "CERA" are not on the same baseline** — "ALEXA" is in the upper-left, "CERA" is in the lower-right. They form a diagonal, not a horizontal line. Do not align them.
- **The headline does not fill the full width** — there is deliberate empty space to the right of "ALEXA." Do not stretch or justify it to fill the container.
- **The radial glow is not perfectly centered** — it sits slightly behind and above the subject's head/shoulders, not mathematically centered on the card. Keep it organic and slightly asymmetric.
- **The photo is not contained** — the subject's body bleeds toward the edges of the composition. Do not add padding or a bounding box to contain it.
- **The CTA button is small relative to the headline** — this contrast in scale is intentional. Do not upsize the button to "balance" the layout.

---

## 3. Micro-Decisions to Preserve

Small choices that carry the character of the design:

- The logo mark is an **abstract dot cluster**, not a letter-based monogram or wordmark. Keep it ambiguous and organic — not geometric-perfect.
- The nav links have **no hover underline or border** by default — they are plain and quiet, letting the orange button do all the work.
- The "HIRE ME" button uses **no icon** — no arrow, no chevron. Just text. Resist the urge to add one.
- The social icons sit **at the bottom of the photo area**, not in the footer of the page. They feel like part of the hero, not an afterthought.
- Body copy is **two short lines**, not a paragraph. Do not expand it. Brevity is part of the voice.
- The cursor icon visible in the design is a **large black pointer** — a deliberate nod to interactivity, placed near the subject's hand. If recreating this, keep it oversized and slightly playful.

---

## 4. The "Do NOT Do This" List

Explicit things to avoid — each one pushes the result toward generic:

| Do NOT | Why it kills the design |
|--------|------------------------|
| Add card borders or box shadows to the hero | Makes it look like a UI component, not a canvas |
| Center-align the headline or body text | Destroys the left-anchored editorial tension |
| Use a gradient background | Replaces the warmth of the flat blush with a tech-startup cliché |
| Add a hero badge or tag (e.g., "Available for hire ✅") | Overused pattern — the button already handles this |
| Use more than one accent color | The single orange is the whole point — diluting it makes it generic |
| Add a grid or card layout to the hero | This is a typographic poster, not a dashboard |
| Animate the headline with a typewriter effect | Overused, undermines the bold static impact |
| Use a stock photo or avatar placeholder | The cut-out personal photo is core to the identity |
| Make the glow a perfect circle with hard edges | Should be a soft radial blur, not a geometric shape |
| Add a hero subtitle tag like "Hi, I'm..." | Conversational openers flatten the boldness |
| Use Inter, Poppins, or Roboto for the headline | These are default AI/template choices — use a display font with personality |
| Add a scroll-down indicator arrow | Breaks the self-contained poster feel of the hero |
| Use white as the background | Loses the warmth that makes the orange feel intentional |

## 5. Tone & Voice Constraints

The written content on the page follows specific tonal rules:

- **No buzzwords** — nothing like "passionate," "innovative," "results-driven," or "crafting digital experiences"
- **No first-person opener** — the hero does not start with "Hi, I'm..." or "Hello, my name is..." The NAME IS the opener
- **Short, declarative sentences** — "I'm a software developer based in Dortmund, Germany." — no elaboration needed in the hero
- **CTA is a command, not a question** — "HIRE ME" not "Want to work together?" or "Let's connect"
- **Name treated as visual object** — the name is not just text, it is the dominant visual element. It should be treated as a graphic, not a label.

---

## 6. Reference Anchors

Use these as directional anchors when making ambiguous decisions:

- **Typography feel:** Swiss/International editorial posters from the 1960s–70s — type as image, not decoration
- **Photo treatment:** Editorial fashion cut-out style — subject removed from background, placed directly on the design surface
- **Color energy:** Inspired by Dutch design / Wim Crouwel school — bold primary/secondary color used sparingly but dominantly
- **Layout logic:** Think poster-first, not webpage-first — if it would look good printed at A2 size, the layout is working
- **Interaction philosophy:** The design should feel complete and resolved at rest — no design element should "need" animation to make sense

---

## 7. The 20% That Makes It Human

These are the hardest things to replicate — the fingerprints of the original design:

1. **Scale relationships feel slightly wrong on purpose** — the name is too big, the button is too small, the subtitle is almost invisible. This hierarchy breaks conventional rules but creates unmistakable personality.
2. **The photo and type are in conversation** — "ALEXA" introduces, the photo responds, "CERA" completes. It's a sentence, not a layout.
3. **Nothing is trying to be liked** — there are no rounded-corner containers, no friendly micro-copy, no social proof numbers. It's confident to the point of being indifferent to approval.
4. **The orange is earned** — it only appears where it means something (logo, name, button, prop). Every use of orange has a reason. Do not add orange anywhere it doesn't already exist.
5. **The blush background does nothing** — it exists purely to be warm and get out of the way. Any background that "does something" (texture, pattern, gradient) is competing with the design.
